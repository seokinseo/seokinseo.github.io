Research Interests

Reinforcement LearningImitation LearningPhysical AI

My research lies at the intersection of reinforcement learning and imitation learning, with the broad goal of building sequential decision-making agents that remain reliable when the data they learn from is imperfect. During my Ph.D., I worked on offline imitation learning that is robust to covariate shift and data collection biases (DrilDICE, NeurIPS’24; PALR, NeurIPS’23; DemoDICE, ICLR’22), and more recently on learning safe behavior without hand-specified cost functions (DexDICE, NeurIPS’26).

At KIST, I am extending this line of work toward physical AI, where these methods meet real robots such as humanoids, manipulators, and mobile robots.
